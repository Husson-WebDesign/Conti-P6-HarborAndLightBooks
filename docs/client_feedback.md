# Client Feedback and Redesign Brief
## Harbor Light Books & Stationery

**Website under review:** https://husson-webdesign.github.io/Conti-P6-HarborAndLightBooks/

Hello,

Thank you for sharing the first version of our website. The deep teal, soft green, and warm accent color are a promising foundation for a bookstore with a coastal identity. I also like the cozy bookstore image on the About page, the clear service categories, and the frequently asked questions. When I opened the special-order question, its answer appeared as expected.

For the next version, I would like a much more distinctive and complete bookstore experience. Our website should feel as inviting and carefully curated as walking into an independent bookshop. Someone should be able to discover the kinds of books and stationery we offer, learn about our community activities, understand how we help readers, and easily find information for a visit.

The current pages communicate the basics, but they feel sparse. The homepage is largely a single block of centered text; the About page has a large image followed by two brief paragraphs; and our service cards describe useful offerings without guiding visitors toward a next step. I would like you to rethink the entire customer experience, not simply add decoration.

## Phase 1: Define a Distinctive Bookstore Identity

### 1. Develop a memorable visual identity

The lighthouse and coastal palette are a good starting point. Please refine them into a recognizable brand that feels literary, warm, welcoming, and connected to our community.

Create a consistent presentation for our name, logo, colors, headings, body text, buttons, and image treatments. The current black lighthouse is difficult to distinguish against the dark header at the size I viewed; please improve its visibility and make sure the mark works at both large and small sizes.

I would like the design to feel like an independent bookstore with its own personality, not a generic business template. Choose a small set of colors and type styles and use them thoughtfully throughout the website.

### 2. Improve the first impression

The homepage currently repeats our full name in both the header and the main welcome heading. Please use that valuable space to introduce what makes a visit worthwhile: a place to discover books, stationery, and community events in Bangor.

Create a concise, inviting message and a short supporting description. Offer clear next steps, such as **Discover Our Books** and **Plan Your Visit**. The message should feel warm and specific to our shop without making claims that have not been confirmed.

### 3. Make text comfortable to read

The homepage uses white text on a soft green background. Please check its readability carefully, including the smaller paragraphs, and adjust the colors or background as needed. Review contrast throughout the site, including the logo, navigation, buttons, and interactive states.

Use a comfortable reading size and line length. Give important headings, supporting text, and actions distinct visual importance so visitors can understand each page quickly.

## Phase 2: Create a Richer, More Useful Homepage

### 4. Turn the homepage into a welcoming introduction

Please replace the current single-block presentation with a thoughtful sequence of sections. I would like visitors to see:

- An inviting opening image and concise introduction.
- A preview of the kinds of books, stationery, and gifts we offer.
- A small selection of featured reads or categories using approved content.
- A preview of our events or the kinds of gatherings we host.
- A short introduction to the people and purpose behind the shop.
- A clear way to find our location and hours or explore the rest of the site.

These sections should create a natural journey without repeating the full content of the other pages.

### 5. Give customers more than one starting point

Different people visit for different reasons. A parent may be looking for children's books, a reader may want a recommendation, and a visitor may simply want to know where we are. Please make these paths visible without overwhelming the page.

Buttons and links should describe what visitors will find. For example, a link to our events should lead to event information, while a link to a contact form should not imply that an event place or book order has been confirmed.

## Phase 3: Improve Our Pages and Content

### 6. Develop the About page

The existing bookstore photograph creates a warm atmosphere, but the page currently contains only two short paragraphs after the image. Please give the page a fuller story with clear sections about our purpose, the readers we welcome, the kinds of items we curate, and our connection to the community.

Use only approved project information. Ask me for details about our history, staff, local partnerships, or founding date rather than inventing them. If the photograph is illustrative and not our actual shop, identify it honestly in the project documentation and avoid presenting it as proof of our premises.

### 7. Make books and stationery easier to discover

The homepage mentions fiction, mystery, nonfiction, children's books, Maine authors, journals, stationery, and literary gifts, but the current navigation does not provide a dedicated place to explore them.

Please create a **Books & Stationery** page or a clearly named section that introduces these categories and helps customers understand what they may find. If approved titles or products are provided, feature a small curated selection with their names, descriptions, and any confirmed details.

Do not invent inventory, prices, reviews, availability, book-cover permissions, or claims that a particular title is in stock. Clearly label illustrative examples as samples. A featured list should not be presented as a live inventory system.

### 8. Give events a clear home

Community events are important to our identity, yet they are currently described within a service card. Please create an **Events** page or a well-defined section that explains the kinds of activities we host, such as readings, story programs, and book discussions.

If actual event information is not provided, present the types of events as examples and ask me for approved dates, times, locations, age guidance, and registration details. Do not invent an upcoming schedule or imply that a place has been reserved.

### 9. Strengthen the Services page

The three current services—book recommendations, special orders, and community events—are a good foundation. Please make each one more useful by explaining what a customer can expect and providing an appropriate next step.

For example, a special-order inquiry could ask a visitor to prepare a title and author. Do not promise that a book is available, an order is placed, or a delivery date is confirmed. Confirm any pricing, deposits, turnaround times, or policies before adding them.

### 10. Improve visiting information

The Contact page shows Bangor, an example email, a 555 phone number, and operating hours, but it does not provide a street address. Please bring approved location, hours, contact details, and accessibility or parking information together in a clear **Visit Us** area.

Confirm the address, phone number, email, hours, and any holiday changes with me. If details are fictional for the class project, identify them as sample information. Do not invent a street address, map location, accessibility feature, or parking policy.

## Phase 4: Refine Layout and Visual Presentation

### 11. Improve the header and navigation

The current header occupies a large part of the desktop screen before the page content begins. Please make it more compact and balanced while keeping our name recognizable. Make the logo or business name a link to the homepage and keep the current page clearly indicated.

Organize navigation around the things customers want to do: learn about the shop, discover books, explore events, and plan a visit. If you add pages, keep the menu understandable and avoid adding unnecessary links.

### 12. Create a consistent layout system

Please use a consistent content width, section spacing, alignment, and visual rhythm. The current homepage uses centered text, while the Services page uses a wide card grid and the About page uses a large centered image. These can work together, but the relationship between them needs to feel more intentional.

Use a mixture of layouts where they support the content: image and text, featured book cards, event previews, and clear information panels. Avoid repeating the same large centered block on every page. Keep generous space purposeful and make sure sections do not feel unfinished.

### 13. Design stronger cards and actions

The current service cards are tidy, but they end after short descriptions. Please add clear destinations for visitors who want to learn more or prepare an inquiry. Make cards consistent in spacing, text hierarchy, and alignment without forcing every piece of information into the same length.

Provide visible hover and keyboard-focus states. Only make an entire card appear clickable when it truly has a destination.

### 14. Choose images and visual details carefully

Use a small set of high-quality images that support the story: bookshelves, reading, stationery, or community gatherings. Make sure images fit their spaces, load reliably, and do not overwhelm the text.

Use images and book covers only when permitted, document their sources, and provide meaningful descriptions where appropriate. Do not represent stock imagery as our actual shop, staff, inventory, or event without confirmation.

### 15. Make the footer useful

The current footer contains only a copyright notice. Please add approved visiting details, hours, contact information, and links to important pages. Design it as a useful final destination rather than an afterthought, while keeping it readable on a phone.

## Phase 5: Make the Customer Journey Clear

### 16. Help readers find their next book

Please offer a simple way to explore approved featured books or categories, such as searching by title or author or selecting a genre. Visitors should be able to clear their choices and see all examples again. If nothing matches, show a helpful message.

If no approved titles are available, use clearly labeled fictional sample entries or ask me for content. Make it clear that the displayed selection is a demonstration, not live stock or a way to purchase a book.

### 17. Make inquiries honest and useful

The Contact page currently offers a **“Send Message”** form. For this class project, I understand that GitHub Pages will not send or save messages. Please make that limitation clear before the visitor begins and use a label such as **“Preview Inquiry.”**

After required information is completed, show an easy-to-read preview and let the visitor make changes. Offer appropriate inquiry choices, such as a general question, book recommendation, special-order question, or event question. For a book inquiry, ask for the title and author where applicable.

Do not say that a message was sent, an order was placed, stock was checked, a book was reserved, or an event registration was completed. No backend, payment, email service, or live booking system is required for this project.

### 18. Preserve and improve the FAQ

The FAQ is a useful feature. I opened the special-order question and saw its answer appear. Please retain the feature and check that all questions can be opened and closed using a keyboard, that their state is clear, and that answers remain accurate.

Add questions only when the answers are approved. Do not invent policies for special orders, returns, event registration, or availability.

## Phase 6: Make Content Easier to Maintain

### 19. Organize featured content in JSON

I would like a future developer to update featured books or events without searching through multiple pages. Please keep the approved featured information in a separate **JSON file or files** and use it to display the items on the website.

For a sample book collection, each entry could include its title, author, category, short description, and approved image information. If event examples are provided, include their approved title, description, and any confirmed date and location.

Please make sure:

- Sample content is clearly labeled and does not imply live inventory or real upcoming events.
- The featured items shown on the homepage and discovery page are consistent.
- Search or category filters use the same information that appears on the page.
- Visitors see a helpful message if the information cannot be displayed.
- A future developer can find and update the files using the project documentation.
- Missing book details, prices, dates, and permissions are confirmed rather than invented.

## Phase 7: Responsive Design and Accessibility

### 20. Make the website comfortable on every device

Please test the homepage, book discovery, services, events, and contact experience on phone, tablet, and desktop sizes. Text should be readable without zooming, images and cards should fit the screen, controls should be easy to tap, and no page should require sideways scrolling.

If the navigation becomes a compact menu, it should open and close clearly and work with a keyboard. Do not let the header occupy most of a small screen.

### 21. Make the website welcoming to more readers

Please check that visitors can use the site with a keyboard, see which item has focus, understand links without guessing, and read text against its background. Provide appropriate image descriptions and clear labels and error messages for form fields.

Make the FAQ, search, and filters usable without a mouse. Give each page a clear main heading and a meaningful browser title. Confirm that content remains understandable if images or interactive features do not load.

## Phase 8: Final Review and Handoff

### 22. Review the published website

Before presenting the redesign, please check that:

- Every page opens directly and can be reached through the navigation.
- Our business name, approved contact information, and hours are consistent.
- The logo is visible and takes visitors home.
- Images, links, buttons, FAQs, search, and filters work.
- Book and event examples are labeled accurately and do not imply live availability.
- The inquiry form clearly explains that it is a demonstration and does not send or save information.
- JSON-powered content loads on GitHub Pages and shows a helpful message if it fails.
- The site works on phone, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- Keyboard navigation, focus visibility, text contrast, and image descriptions have been reviewed.
- Spelling, grammar, content accuracy, page titles, and image sources are checked.

### 23. Provide a professional handoff

Please provide the published website and repository links, a brief explanation of the redesign, desktop and phone screenshots, image and book-cover sources, known limitations, and a list of details that still need my confirmation. Include instructions explaining how a future developer can update featured books, event examples, and visiting information.
